## Catalog acquisition is the first blocking gate

Legacy rules p2 establish: 110 train cards (12 each of eight colors, plus 14 locomotives), 30 destination tickets, five scoring markers, one longest-path bonus, and 45 playable trains per player. Their 240 physical trains include replacement spares; playable supply is 225, not 240. Summary/promotional cards never enter either playable deck.

Required checked-in data, before rules implementation is accepted:

- `cities`: canonical IDs, printed names/aliases and independently drawn UI coordinates. Expected 36; verify by directly counting the edition-locked board.
- `routes`: one record **per independently claimable lane**, with ID, unordered endpoints, length, color/gray, and parallel-group ID. The commonly reported USA count is 100 lanes, but the official text does not enumerate them. **100 is an acquisition hypothesis, not a certified count from this research.** Do not confuse lane count, unique city pairs, or number of plastic spaces. Do not infer a double's second lane from the first lane's color.
- `tickets`: all 30 IDs, exact city endpoints and printed values, with a card-by-card source ledger. Do not fill missing tickets from memory or import the 33-ticket refresh deck.
- Color inventory: purple, blue, orange, white, green, yellow, black, red, plus locomotives. Use text/pattern labels as well as colors.
- Route points indexed by length: 1→1, 2→2, 3→4, 4→7, 5→10, 6→15 (legacy p4).

Acquire an authorized high-resolution original board/card reference or inspect the physical original box. Two people should independently transcribe and reconcile every lane and ticket; attach source location and SHA-256 to the acquisition ledger. The small setup illustration is not adequate to certify every lane's color/length. Require exact count reconciliation, endpoint validity, parallel-pair review, connectedness, and a visual overlay audit. This is the unresolved map-data blocker; no random GitHub map becomes authoritative merely because it reports 100 routes. USA has ordinary colored/gray routes, without ferry, tunnel, or station mechanics.

## Rules checklist and decision boundaries

Legacy p2: start with four train cards; offer three tickets, retaining at least two; expose five train cards. The publisher-authored 2004 manual p3 also specifies: choose draw, claim, or ticket draw. Refill face-up draws immediately; an exposed locomotive consumes the draw turn, but a blind locomotive counts once. Second exposed locomotive is unavailable. Three exposed locomotives flush the market. Recycle train discards; no train supply means choose another action. Claims pay one color plus wilds, match length, consume trains, and need not extend an existing network. A player cannot own both parallel lanes; with 2–3 players claiming either closes its mate. P4: ticket draws offer up to three, retain at least one; returns go underneath.

Final scoring authority: [publisher 2024 rules p4](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-daysofwonder/uploads/2024/05/7201-T2R-Rules-EN-20240423_LOWRES.pdf). Trigger at ≤2 remaining trains; everyone, including the trigger player, gets one subsequent turn. Tickets contribute signed values. Longest trail awards 10 to every tied longest player. Cities may repeat; train segments may not. Score ties compare completed-ticket count, then longest-bonus ownership.

After all published score tiebreaks remain equal, return shared winners with equal winner shares. This is the explicit application draw default for a residual tie not broken by the inspected rule text; it invents no extra ranking statistic.

Rare unresolved transitions must be explicit acceptance blockers: a market that cannot stabilize below three locomotives with the remaining supply; zero tickets; only one drawable train card; and a position with no legal action. The legacy booklet does not completely prescribe every exhausted-supply deadlock. Seek an official clarification, retain the query/evidence, and encode a documented ruling; never invent an ordinary pass, reshuffle tickets, or loop forever. Concrete interim engine policy: enter `phase:'supplyAdjudication'` with `{reason, pendingTransaction, observedSupply, legalAlternativeActions}` when the selected draw/refill cannot be resolved under the pinned rules. Preserve the pre-transaction snapshot; `applyAction` accepts only cancellation/correction or an explicit administrative `recordAdjudication{policyId, evidence, outcome}` in this phase. Cancellation restores the snapshot and permits any available ordinary alternative; an adjudication records table provenance before resuming. Do not add a normal pass, pick a winner, or fabricate a draw. Fully automatic official games reaching this phase are marked unresolved/non-complete, never benchmark draws. Before reaching it, a known impossible zero-supply draw is omitted while other legal actions remain available. A physical correction can record an agreed table ruling without presenting it as canonical.

## Exact scoring and graph model

Use an undirected **multigraph** with stable lane IDs. Ownership is per lane; availability accounts for both player count and parallel-group ownership. Ticket completion uses only the player's claimed routes, via BFS/union-find. It is connectivity, not a requirement to follow a unique shortest route.

Longest-path scoring must be an exact maximum-weight **trail**, not graph diameter, a simple path, an MST, or total connected-component size. Weight is lane length. Enumerate trails from every incident city with an edge-used bitset; allow city revisits, forbid edge reuse. Connected components and admissible remaining-edge bounds can prune safely. An Eulerian component can consume all edges when it has zero or two odd-degree vertices; more odd vertices require choosing which edges to omit. Branches do not all count automatically. Memoize `(currentCity, usedEdges)` where useful and return a witness lane sequence. Final scoring cannot silently time out into a heuristic. If a performance budget is exceeded, show pending exact computation rather than a false official result. Search evaluation may use a clearly named approximation; final adjudication may not.

## State, actions and observation contract

Game-local ownership: `ticket-to-ride/{catalog,core,scoring,actions,observations,belief,ai,worker,app}.js`, `ticket-to-ride/index.html` and `ticket-to-ride/styles.css`, following COMMON. Optional internal analysis/physical-UI modules do not replace those stable entry points. A game owner owns these files and focused tests. The parent alone integrates shared shell, navigation, static-assets, packaging and cross-game documentation.

True state stores edition/catalog hashes, seat order, turn phase, train/destination deck order, discards, face-up slots, hands, private tickets, route ownership, remaining trains, and immutable final-round trigger/countdown. Model drawing as substeps: choose first source, record actual draw and refill/flush observations, then choose a legal second source. A preselected pair cannot assume the replacement card is already known. Ticket offers similarly enter a private keep-selection phase.

Canonical actions must represent every legal claim payment: each gray-route base color and each feasible locomotive substitution, including all-wild payment, with no duplicate semantic payments. Enumerate every valid ticket-retention subset and first/second draw source. Human recording uses this complete legal surface. Analysis can prioritize equivalent/less promising payments only after legality is complete, and must expose pruning counts.

Actor projections expose public claims, trains, market, discard information, public draw events, hand/ticket counts, and only the actor's private cards/tickets/offers. Public face-up purchases remain remembered evidence; blind draws and rejected/kept opponent ticket identities remain unknown. Do not send true deck order or opponent tickets to a worker and then trust the worker to ignore them. Belief particles satisfy physical conservation and recorded events; opponent destination probabilities may be inferred from routes without becoming facts. The physical log stores what was observed, with explicit unknowns and provenance, rather than a sampled world disguised as history.

## Physical workflow and interface

Choose original USA edition and player count before loading the catalog. Provide a legible vector map, selectable parallel lanes, color/pattern legend, train counts, private own hand/tickets, and an event timeline. A claim form specifies lane and actual payment. Drawing asks for actual card identities and market refills; ticket offers stay in a privacy panel. Analysis, hover and variations cannot commit moves. Confirm, cancel, undo, correction, save/reload, and atomic recovery must work through partial draw/refill/ticket phases. Unknown cards remain unknown after reload. Physical corrections revalidate conservation and invalidate affected analysis; they must not silently rewrite unrelated events.

## Affordable AI and evidence plan

Begin with transparent hand-built evaluation: route points, ticket completion cost/risk, bottleneck urgency, overlap of ticket networks, useful color demand, locomotive opportunity cost and endgame train tempo. Distinguish estimated hidden ticket pressure from observed objectives. A worker samples consistent hidden worlds, compares legal root actions using short rollouts, and progressively allocates samples; report time, sample count, root coverage and pruning. Use exact ticket connectivity and exact final trail scoring at terminal nodes. Avoid an unconstrained opponent-hand oracle or a costly global neural model.

Test plan: independent catalog audit first; exhaustive small-payment legality; deterministic transition/replay/conservation tests; hidden-state metamorphic tests; and exact scoring fixtures. Hand fixtures include a length-3 and length-4 chain (route score 11), a completed +7 ticket and failed −5 ticket (net +2), a three-spoke length-2 star (longest trail 4, not 6), and a triangle of length-2 edges with a length-1 tail (trail 7, with a repeated city). Exercise parallel closures at 2/3 players versus 4/5, insufficient trains, wildcard-only gray payments, final trigger seat wrapping, equal longest trails, score tiebreaks (including equal score/completed-ticket count/bonus ownership→equal winner shares), each supply-adjudication entry/cancel/reload path, reshuffles and the blocked-supply rulings above. Property-test tiny multigraph results against a separate exhaustive trail enumerator.

Benchmark only after complete legal-action and scoring gates pass. Use deterministic recorded seed lists, rotate all seats/start positions, and separate 2/3/4/5-player results. Compare random legal, simple route/ticket heuristic and sampled search at matched time budgets. Predeclare budget and stopping rules; reserve fresh holdout seeds, report paired margins/ranks/win shares with intervals, invalid/capped games, latency and root coverage. Multiplayer samples are independent games/seed groups, not independent seat results. Preserve source/catalog hashes and raw results. Strength evidence cannot substitute for exact rules or physical usability.
